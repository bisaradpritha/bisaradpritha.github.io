My Research Journey

Research Introduction

I am a computational biologist and neuroscientist driven by a fascination with how genetic variation shapes the developing nervous system and influences human disease. At Phoenix Children's, my research combines genomics, transcriptomics, and computational biology to uncover the molecular causes of pediatric movement disorders, including cerebral palsy and dystonia. I also investigate how neural signals recorded during deep brain stimulation can reveal the dynamic activity of brain circuits and help guide more personalized therapies. By integrating multi-omics, bioinformatics, and machine learning, I aim to bridge molecular discovery with clinical translation and contribute to more precise diagnoses and treatments for children with complex neurological disorders.

My research began at the intersection of molecular biology and artificial intelligence. During my doctoral training, I investigated the binding behavior of monoclonal antibodies using high-throughput random peptide microarrays, demonstrating that machine learning models could accurately predict antibody binding motifs from randomly generated peptide sequences alone. This work deepened my interest in developing computational approaches to uncover hidden biological patterns, a philosophy that continues to shape my research in genomics, multi-omics, and neuroscience today.
